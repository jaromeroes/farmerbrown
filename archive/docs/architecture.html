Internal — Farmer Brown Insurance · v4.0 unified architecture

Inbound Call Flow
& Agent Routing

A tiered routing system across three websites. Each site has one toll-free number and one receptionist who triages every call into one of four explicit intents at the entry point — new quote, existing quote, existing policy, or speak with someone specific. Spanish callers are detected at any point and transferred to a dedicated Spanish team. New quotes flow to product specialists for instant quotes or appointments; existing quotes (hot leads) route to their own line; service intents reach a dedicated service team after an inline COI flow; named-person requests resolve via an internal directory of direct numbers.

· status · v4.0 unified architecture · one receptionist per site
All three websites run the same unified receptionist that triages every inbound call into one of four explicit intents, then routes via five dedicated outbound destinations (Sales specialists, Service team, Existing-Quote team, Spanish team, direct-dial directory). The flow diagrams in section 01 (call flow) and section 03 (COI) describe the path inside the receptionist — identical on all three sites. The architecture also assumes a "lean by default" stance on the receptionist's instructions: only the live behavioural content is sent to the model on every turn; version history and unused metadata live in companion files.

· path.a · new quote / shopping
Routes to the Sales path — specialist routing by product (Builder's Risk, GL, WC, Commercial Auto, Home & Auto). Instant quote on call when applicable. See section 01.
· path.b · existing quote / following up hot lead
Caller already received a quote and is following up to close. Receptionist transfers immediately to a dedicated Existing-Quote team line — these are 5× more valuable than service calls and bypass the standard menu.
· path.c · existing policy / service
Routes to the Service path — closed-menu triage (COI, payment, claim, other). COI is AI-handled in-call (see section 03); every other intent transfers to a dedicated Service team line.
· path.d · speak with someone in particular
Receptionist looks up the named person in an internal directory of ~20 team members with their direct numbers. Unique match → direct-dial. Ambiguous (e.g. "John") → disambiguate, then direct-dial. No match → transfer to live agent with the captured name in the call summary.
· cross-cutting · spanish detection
Independent of the four paths above. If the caller speaks Spanish, asks for Spanish, or starts the call in Spanish, the receptionist acknowledges in English ("Of course — let me connect you with our Spanish-speaking team.") and transfers to a dedicated Spanish team line. The first-message also proactively offers the option so Spanish-preferring callers self-identify on their first turn. No mid-conversation language switch on the receptionist itself.
01 / unified_call_flow

Every site runs the same internal flow inside a single unified receptionist. Farmer Brown (Emma), Contractors Liability (Olivia), and Builders Risk (Grace) each handle the four intents below in one continuous conversation — no separate phone lines, no separate agents per intent. Spanish callers are routed out to a dedicated team line at any point in the call. The diagram below describes the flow from the moment the receptionist picks up.

tier.a · receptionist picks up · triage question
"Hi there! Thanks for calling [site name]. Are you looking for a new quote, following up on a quote we already sent you, do you need help with an existing policy, or would you like to speak with someone in particular? And if you'd prefer to be helped in Spanish, just let me know."
The same receptionist hears the answer and stays on the call — no handoff to another agent. The answer determines which of the four branches below runs next. Spanish detection is cross-cutting and short-circuits to the Spanish team line at any point.

branch.04 · speak with someone in particular
Trigger: "can I speak with [name]", "is [name] available", "I'm looking for [name]".
internal directory lookup
Receptionist consults an internal directory of ~20 team members (full names, first-name aliases, extensions, direct numbers, direct-dial status flag). Three sub-branches:
· Unique match — "Of course — connecting you to [full name]. One moment." Direct-dial.
· Ambiguous (e.g. "John") — disambiguate first, then direct-dial.
· No match — capture name verbatim, transfer to live agent with the name in the call summary so the human can redirect.
coverage
Directory updated by editing one file + re-running the deployment script. New team members can be added without changing the receptionist's prompt.
tier.c · dedicated outbound destinations · one per intent class
Instead of routing everything that escapes the AI into a single "live agent" pool, the receptionist routes to five distinct human-staffed lines, each tuned to the intent that reaches it. The receiving team knows the call context before picking up.
destination reaches it signal to the receiving team
Sales specialists New-quote callers, product determined. Caller already qualified for the right product specialist.
Existing-Quote team Caller is following up on a quote already sent. Hot lead — closer should pick up fast and aim to close.
Service team Payment, claim, other-service intents; explicit "live agent" inside the Service branch. Existing customer needing operational help, not a sales opportunity.
Spanish team Any Spanish-detected caller at any point in the conversation. Spanish-speaking team takes over the call from a clean slate.
Direct-dial / live agent Specific-person request resolves to a directory entry → direct number; no match → generic live-agent line with the captured name. Either reaches the named person directly, or reaches a human who can find them.
tier.d · graceful escalation · applies to every agent
Confusion / stuck: at any point, if the receptionist (or a downstream specialist) is confused or stuck, the call is warm-transferred to the appropriate team line above — Service team if the confusion happened inside the Service branch, Sales live-agent otherwise.
Silence timeout: if the caller goes silent for ~20 seconds at any point, the agent proactively asks "Are you still there? Would you like me to connect you with a live agent?" — and transfers immediately if the caller asks or stays silent. Applies to every agent in every situation.
The system never insists on automation when a human is the right answer.

Confirm caller identity using the phone number on file. If different, ask for business name and the phone on file.

Collect name, street, city, state, ZIP of the party to be listed on the certificate. Slow readback to confirm before continuing.

Walk through three common options — waiver of subrogation, primary & non-contributory, products & completed operations — one by one.

Capture a phone or email for the additional insured when available, so the certificate can be sent directly.

Standard 24h. Expedited 1h offered as a quid pro quo: a review from the caller in return. If accepted: SMS review link + urgent internal alert.
step.06 · cross-sell
Ask about Home & Auto. If yes, promise an SMS application link — does not hand off to Rachel, closes the call in place.

07 / design_principles
· single entry, many paths
Every caller on every site lands on one number, one bilingual receptionist, and one triage question — sales, service, or Spanish — before the flow branches into the appropriate path. Predictable triage, zero dead ends.
· specialists, not generalists
Each product has its own specialist with its own script, tone, and data requirements. A Builder's Risk caller never feels like they're filling out a Home & Auto form.
· audible handoffs
Receptionists and specialists use distinct voices, so the caller audibly recognizes when they've been connected to the right expert — not just a different prompt.
· graceful escalation
Any caller who is confused, frustrated, or asking for a person is routed to a licensed agent within seconds. A 7-second silence on the caller's side automatically triggers a "would you like a live agent?" prompt. The system never insists on automation when a human is the right answer.
